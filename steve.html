My hero

Steve Backshall, the man who made me love sharks.

Three years ago I watched him swim next to a great white with no cage, looking completely calm, and something in my brain just went "oh". Sharks weren't the monsters. This page is about him, and why he's the reason this website exists.

Human beings will only ever look after the things they value. Our first battle then is to convince the world of the wonder of sharks.
Steve Backshall, when he became patron of Bite-Back in 2016
Who he is

Explorer, presenter, shark defender.

Steve Backshall was born in Bagshot in Surrey in 1973 and grew up on a smallholding full of rescued animals, which probably explains a lot. He studied English and theatre at Exeter, went off to try and walk across New Guinea on his own (he didn't make it, but he got a TV job out of it), and ended up as National Geographic's "Adventurer in Residence" at 25.

Most people my age know him from Deadly 60, which started in 2008 and is still the best wildlife programme ever made, I will not be taking questions. But he's also led proper expeditions to places nobody had been, climbed mountains, kayaked rivers, written loads of books, done Strictly, and got an MBE for his conservation work. He's married to Helen Glover, who has two Olympic gold medals for rowing, and they live in Cornwall with their three kids.

Steve and sharks

He's been in the water with nearly all of them.

This is the bit that got me. He doesn't just talk about sharks from a boat. He gets in.

No cage

Great whites, cage-free

In Swimming with Monsters he swam next to great white sharks with nothing between him and them. He's done it since too. He says it was "a beautiful, enriching experience", which is not what most people would say.

Bitten

Once, in a chain-mail suit

Filming Deadly Pole to Pole a shark did bite him, but he was wearing a chain-mail shark suit so it didn't get through. He carried on filming. Obviously.

5 species

On Deadly 60 alone

Great white, bull shark, great hammerhead, mako and tiger shark, all outside the cage. Those are some of the sharks people are most scared of, and he showed millions of kids they weren't monsters.

3 charities

Patron for sharks and rays

He's patron of Bite-Back, the Shark Trust and the Manta Trust. That's the fin trade, UK shark science and manta rays all covered.

Why he's my hero

Three things I've learnt from him.

Being scared isn't a reason not to look closer

He's honest about the animals being dangerous. He just doesn't let that be the end of the story. The more you learn about a shark, the less scary it gets and the more amazing it gets.

Say the true thing, even if it's not the fun thing

He calls shark finning "the most baffling and frustrating cause in conservation". He could just do the exciting diving bits and leave it there. He doesn't.

Actually do something

Patron of charities, raising money, going on telly to talk about fin bans. He and Helen raised £360,000 for the World Land Trust in one year. This website is my version of that. Much smaller, but it's a start.
